# XSched three-way performance comparison on NVIDIA 575

The fixed short-budget campaign completed **5 randomized complete blocks / 15
valid cells**, with zero failed or excluded cells. Original upstream XSched
Level-1 HPF, native CUDA, and our gpubpf timeslice/preemption policy all ran on
the same RTX 5090, Linux `6.15.11-061511-generic`, and custom NVIDIA `575.57.08`
stack. The result does **not** support a gpubpf LC-latency advantage in this
configuration: XSched has lower LC latency, while gpubpf has modestly higher
BE throughput than XSched.

## Measured performance

Values are medians of five per-cell statistics, not a pooled single run.
LC latency is host submission to first device entry; lower is better.

| Configuration | LC P50 (ms) | LC P95 (ms) | LC P99 / maximum (ms) | BE kernels/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Native CUDA | 3113.397 | 6673.535 | 7245.487 | 10.2091 |
| Original XSched Level-1 HPF | 2447.267 | 4094.335 | 4293.025 | 9.5744 |
| Our gpubpf priority policy | 3056.812 | 6723.899 | 7396.935 | 9.8842 |

Fixed-seed 10,000-draw paired bootstrap intervals for mean differences:

- gpubpf minus XSched LC P99: **+2618.222 to +3546.548 ms** (95% CI).
- gpubpf relative BE throughput versus XSched: **+3.277% to +4.350%**.
- gpubpf minus native LC P99: **+3.202 to +267.325 ms**.
- gpubpf relative BE throughput versus native: **−3.500% to −0.856%**.

The predeclared LC-improvement plus BE-noninferiority decision is `negative`.
This is a scoped result for this policy/workload, not proof that every gpubpf
scheduling policy is worse than XSched. No policy was tuned after inspecting
the comparative timings, and no unfavorable cell was discarded.

## Scope and protocol

- XSched upstream revision: `f49289f0220931df78de948ed841ecbaf960a919`, with the
  small passive suspend/resume audit patch checked by exact source edits.
- XSched runner source revision: `1f50495`; all commands used the already
  built shared harness and gpubpf loaders recorded in admission inventories.
- Two LC and four BE processes, four streams per process, five kernels per
  stream, 340 blocks × 256 threads per kernel; no memory oversubscription.
- Native isolated calibration froze **9,496,464 FMA repetitions** at
  **80.023776 ms** per kernel before comparative execution.
- XSched uses its original global HPF scheduler, Level-1, LC priority/
  threshold/batch `1/16/8`, BE `0/4/2`.
- gpubpf uses LC/BE timeslices `1,000,000/200 us`, and LC-launch-triggered
  BE preemption with a 100 us cooldown.
- Complete blocks are randomized with seed 1797. All configurations use the
  same harness, CPU allocation, BE-first release, and 5 ms LC-release delay.
- Each cell contains **40 LC samples and 80 BE kernels**. With only 40 LC
  samples, nearest-rank P99 equals the maximum; it is **not** a high-confidence
  steady-state P99 estimate. Across five blocks each configuration contributes
  200 LC samples and 400 BE kernels.

This is real repeated three-way performance measurement, not just attachment
or build smoke. It is nevertheless a deliberately smaller campaign than the
original **50 kernels/stream × 10 complete blocks + isolated role controls**.
That original full protocol remains incomplete. It also does not reproduce
the XSched paper's Level-3 hardware-preemption results: this public sm_120
configuration is Level-1.

## Correctness, engagement, and safety

Every worker verified every output against its exact 32-lane CPU recurrence:
**156,672,000 values** across the 15 timed cells, with no mismatch. All 1,800
kernels completed with expected block counts and valid timestamp ordering.

Every XSched cell independently recorded 24 unique Level-1 XQueues, including
all 16 BE queues with one successful suspend and one resume (80 of each across
five cells). Every gpubpf cell recorded 20 timeslice modifications and eight
successful preemption calls, with zero preemption errors. These are actual
policy-engagement observations, not proof that a successful API call alone
guarantees a particular hardware-preemption latency.

All 15 post-cell observations show GPU utilization 0%, UVM reference count 0,
empty struct-ops maps/links, no Xid or kernel abnormality, and the active 400 W
power limit. Exclusive GPU/struct-ops leases prevented overlap. Cleanup only
signaled the runner's recorded child process groups. The maximum measured
pre/post clock-offset drift plus bracket uncertainty was **0.257438 ms**, far
below the reported multi-second latency differences.
